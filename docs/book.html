The Book

When the Blueprint Burned

A Unicorn Mama Memoir of Neurodiversity, Strength, Joy & Humor

A son who changed everything. A daughter who reminded her what matters most.

When Jennifer's toddler lined up tomatoes with the precision of a diamond cutter and crawled straight for the wall, everyone said wait. Her gut said run. The diagnosis (autism, with OCD and ADHD riding shotgun) burned the blueprint she'd been sketching since the second pink line.

What she built instead is the story of this book: three decades of IEP battles and grocery-store meltdowns, a vanishing wedding ring, a Christmas spent in the bathroom, a gruff German doctor, an AAC breakthrough over bacon and eggs, and a love so fierce it made her feel like a unicorn.

For the weary-hearted, curled up in the dark at 2 a.m.

Welcome to the Club No One Applied For

I didn't sign up for this.
Neither did you.
And yet, here we are.

This life is hard to explain to people who haven't lived it. If you haven't worn the backpack, you don't know the weight. The bone-deep exhaustion. The endless juggling of therapies, IEPs, appointments. The whiplash of celebrating a tiny victory one minute… and managing a public meltdown the next.

But if you've walked even a few miles on this road, you get it. You know the secret language. You know silence isn't always peaceful. Sometimes it's terrifying. You know that a good poop can be cause for a full-blown family celebration.

So pour yourself a cup of coffee. (You'll probably have to microwave it three times before you finish it, and that's okay.) Take a breath.

Welcome to the club.
We've been waiting for you.

— Jennifer

Read an excerpt

“If laughter and truth can hold hands, this book is their love story.”

Why “Unicorn”? Because this life is rare. Unique. And to the outside world, it sometimes looks like a myth. Being a Unicorn Mama means deciding to find the magic in the mess: glitter in your hair (sometimes literal), a horn on your head when you need to protect your child, and a heart stretched open to a love most people will never fully understand.

“This is not a medical textbook. I am not a doctor. I am a mom who has been in the trenches for thirty years, through diaper blowouts, school expulsions, divorce, health scares, and the quiet (and not-so-quiet) judgment of strangers.”

“Hold on, Mama. The world didn't end. It just shifted, and somehow, you're going to learn how to walk beautifully in this new gravity.”

Sparkle & Sanity Tips

A mindset tip and a practical tip after every chapter, “usually written after bedtime and before the third cup of coffee.”

☕ Sanity Tip: “When someone says ‘Everything happens for a reason,’ you have my full permission to smile politely and then go scream into a pillow.”

Micro-Missions

One small, doable action to take today. No 90-minute yoga class required.
